Students will be able to:

  1. 01Model “3 times as many” stories with number sentences.
  2. 02Solve with at least two different methods.
  3. 03Chain comparisons (fewer than / times as many).

Core Concepts

Problem solving with multiplicative comparison — show two methods.

“Times as Many”

34 rabbit lanterns; ball lanterns = $3\times$ rabbits → total and difference.

Two Methods

Method I and Method II for the same question (e.g. $34+34\times3$ vs $34\times4$).

Chained Comparisons

Stamps: Dingding $24$ (= Ya − 8); Pang = $3\times$ Ya → find Pang’s stamps.

Key Answers

Quick reference for this lesson’s exercise answers.

Key answers
  1. Diagrams: (1) $7\times5+7=42$ boxes;\; (2) $9\times2+7=25$ lilies
  2. Storybooks: $48\times3+15=159$
  3. Father’s age: $11\times4-5=39$
  4. Lanterns: (1) $34\times3+34=136$;\; (2) $102-34=68$ more ball lanterns
  5. Ages: (1) Mum $9\times4=36$;\; (2) Xiao Ming $36\div4=9$
  6. Stamps: Ya $24+8=32$; Pang $32\times3=96$
  7. Science books: $36\times4=144$ copies
  8. Trees by lake: (1) sycamore+willow $730+360=1090$;\; (2) $20$ fewer camphor than sycamore
  9. Enhancement: willow $289$; sycamore $576$ (pattern W–S–S)
